Terracotta Roof Tiles & Pitch Roof

Terracotta roof tiles became a common feature of Katong–Joo Chiat shophouses during the late 19th and early 20th centuries, influenced by Chinese and European building traditions. The steeply pitched roof was designed to cope with Singapore's tropical climate by allowing heavy rainwater to drain quickly while keeping the building cooler.

Plasterwork Festoons & Relief Frieze

Plasterwork festoons and relief friezes became popular during the Early and Late Shophouse styles, reflecting the influence of Victorian and Neoclassical architecture introduced during the British colonial period. These decorative features displayed the owner's wealth and added an elegant, artistic character to the building's façade.

Corinthian / Composite Pilasters

Corinthian and Composite pilasters were inspired by classical Greek and Roman architecture and became popular in Singapore during the late 19th and early 20th centuries through British colonial design. They gave shophouses a grand and symmetrical appearance while showcasing the blend of Western architectural styles with local building traditions.

French Louvred Timber Shutters

French louvred timber shutters were introduced through European colonial influence and became a defining feature of many pre-war shophouses in Katong–Joo Chiat. Their adjustable wooden slats allowed natural ventilation and light while protecting interiors from the tropical sun and rain.

Glazed Peranakan Majolica Tiles

Glazed Peranakan Majolica tiles were widely imported from Britain, Belgium, and Japan during the late 19th and early 20th centuries as decorative building materials. Their colourful floral and geometric designs reflected Peranakan culture and prosperity, while their glazed surface made them durable and easy to clean.

Residential Forecourt & Cast-Iron Gates

The residential forecourt was introduced as a small buffer between the street and the home, providing space for light, ventilation, and social interaction in early shophouse communities. Ornate cast-iron gates, which became fashionable during the colonial era, offered security while displaying decorative craftsmanship that complemented the elegant façades of Katong–Joo Chiat shophouses.
