A Walk Through Time

1819

Tanjong Katong Marked as a Boundary

Tanjong Katong becomes the eastern boundary of Stamford Raffles' British settlement, taking its name from the now-extinct Katong sea turtle once found along the coast.

1820s

Plantations and Kampongs

The land is largely given over to coconut and cotton plantations, dotted with kampongs and seaside bungalows.

1900 – 1910

The Rise of Chew Joo Chiat

Wealthy trader and philanthropist Chew Joo Chiat acquires large plots of land for plantations, earning him the title "King of Katong."

1917

Joo Chiat Road Opens

A major dirt track through his property is opened to the public and becomes known as Joo Chiat Road.

1920 – 1930

Eastward Expansion

Wealthy communities move eastward out of the city centre, building the iconic Peranakan shophouses and seaside villas that remain today.

1942 – 1945

Wartime Pause

Development pauses during the Japanese Occupation, as daily life across Singapore is upended.

1950s – 1970s

Post-War Recovery

Post-war recovery brings department stores, cinemas (like Roxy and Odeon), and entertainment spots. The area transforms into a vibrant residential and commercial suburb.

1991 – 1993

Conservation Begins

The Urban Redevelopment Authority gazettes over 500 buildings for conservation, protecting the area's unique shophouse architecture.

2004

A Community Pushes Back

Residents form the “Save Joo Chiat Working Group” to push back against rising vice and nightlife issues, restoring a family-friendly neighbourhood.

2011

Singapore's First Heritage Town

The area is officially designated Singapore's first Heritage Town, becoming a cultural hub for food, boutique hotels, and tourism.
